# Source Patterns

This document records recurring composition patterns only. It intentionally avoids source marketing copy and product-specific semantics.

## 1. Hero composition

### Anatomy

- A pale blueprint-like field sits behind the opening composition.
- Peripheral geometric forms enter from the edges: thin tubes, circular arcs, faint grid lines, or oversized glyph-like objects.
- A centered heavy headline is enclosed by a lime one-pixel construction frame with small square nodes at its corners.
- A short row of lime-tinted metadata capsules follows.
- A compact paired CTA group leads into one very large framed proof object.

### Visual purpose

To establish the system immediately as confident, constructed, and artifact-led rather than illustration-led.

### Hierarchy

1. Headline mass.
2. Marker-like highlighted phrase within the headline.
3. Lime construction frame and metadata row.
4. Primary action.
5. Large proof object.

### Spacing behavior

The composition is centered with a deliberately broad top field. The CTA row is tightly grouped; the proof object is separated by a much larger vertical gap. The background geometry can touch viewport edges but does not crowd the headline.

### When it works

Use for one central proposition with a convincing visual artifact to substantiate it.

### What breaks it

Multiple competing hero cards, gradient backdrops, a weak/small headline, replacing the construction frame with generic decoration, or placing the primary CTA inside the same visual mass as the headline.

## 2. Utility label + oversized headline

### Anatomy

- Tiny uppercase label with generous tracking.
- One oversized, tight, heavy headline.
- Optional restrained lead paragraph or italic word interruption.
- A fine rule, index, or small metadata mark may anchor the label.

### Visual purpose

Creates editorial seriousness without adding a separate display font or ornamental masthead.

### Hierarchy

The label is a coordinate, not a subtitle. The headline holds the visual weight; body copy is distinctly quieter.

### Spacing behavior

Label-to-headline spacing is intentional but short. Headline-to-supporting copy is larger. The system does not stack multiple large type styles in the same block.

### When it works

For section starts, manifesto statements, or a transition into a stage.

### What breaks it

Large uppercase eyebrow text, a lightweight headline, lengthy paragraphs before the headline, or a second equally large heading adjacent to it.

## 3. Highlighted text treatment

### Anatomy

- Black or charcoal word/phrase.
- A bright lime marker band sits behind the lower portion of the glyphs, or a controlled lime emphasis is applied to a selected phrase.
- The treatment remains inline with normal text rather than becoming a separate badge.

### Visual purpose

Adds a hand-applied editorial jolt while preserving the page’s precise construction logic.

### Hierarchy

The lime is an emphasis marker, not the main text color. It should redirect the eye once, then release it back to charcoal copy.

### Spacing behavior

No extra box or card padding is required. The highlight follows the line box and can wrap cleanly with the text.

### When it works

To emphasize one clause, outcome, number, or key noun inside a larger heading.

### What breaks it

Highlighting multiple phrases in the same heading, turning entire headings lime, using a soft pastel marker, or rendering the lime as a wide generic gradient.

## 4. Construction-grid framing

### Anatomy

- Pale technical grid, arcs, and circles in the background.
- Fine one-pixel rules.
- Bright lime edge marks, crosshairs, or small square nodes at construction corners.
- Occasional dashed circles, numerical indices, or corner brackets.

### Visual purpose

Makes a marketing page feel like a design workbench: the composition is visibly measured, positioned, and testable.

### Hierarchy

Construction lines must remain subordinate to the content. The lime node/frame can briefly rise above the text as an accent, but should not become an independent illustration.

### Spacing behavior

Geometry occupies the outer field and edge zones. Leave the central reading field clean.

### When it works

For a hero, stage, or section transition needing an architectural anchor.

### What breaks it

Dark grids, dense CAD texture, placing grid lines over legibility-critical copy, or scattering lime shapes with no relation to the content bounds.

## 5. Peripheral geometric objects

### Anatomy

- A partial 3D-looking tube, ring, miniature globe, hand-drawn label, icon orbit, or rotated rectangle.
- The object is cropped by the viewport or staged just beyond a content rail.
- It is visually lighter or smaller than the main proof artifact.

### Visual purpose

Creates movement and scale without filling the page with illustrations.

### Hierarchy

Peripheral objects are accents. The page should still read correctly if they are ignored.

### Spacing behavior

Keep them in the margins, behind content, or in the larger white pause between sections. Preserve a clean safe zone around text and controls.

### When it works

For reinforcing a section’s construction/studio feeling or balancing an asymmetric editorial/product split.

### What breaks it

Centering the decorative object as the hero, using many unrelated ornaments, or making it compete with the page’s key artifact.

## 6. Product-stage framing

### Anatomy

- A large, deliberately proportioned image or interface proof.
- One surrounding stage: pale field, colorful artwork, dark enclosure, or white framed window.
- Thin border, moderated radius, and broad shadow.
- Optional caption slab or dock placed inside the stage bounds.

### Visual purpose

Turns a product image into the evidence of the page rather than a decorative screenshot.

### Hierarchy

The stage is usually the visual peak of its section. Supporting controls and captions are legible but smaller.

### Spacing behavior

The stage expands beyond ordinary text width. Its edge relationship matters more than a fixed card width.

### When it works

When a product or artifact can carry visual credibility at large scale.

### What breaks it

Dropping an unframed screenshot into a standard card, cropping away interface context, or surrounding the stage with multiple competing CTA panels.

## 7. Metadata / tag row

### Anatomy

- Several small, fully rounded capsules.
- Pale lime-tinted fills, dark text, compact type, and no heavy borders.
- Simple horizontally wrapped row beneath a main statement.

### Visual purpose

Conveys quick proof points or attributes without interrupting the visual momentum.

### Hierarchy

Tags are supportive evidence. They are more vivid than body copy but distinctly less important than a heading or CTA.

### Spacing behavior

Use small consistent gaps. On narrow screens, allow the row to wrap naturally into compact centered lines.

### When it works

For a finite set of short, scannable qualifiers.

### What breaks it

Long sentences, too many tags, multiple accent colors, strong borders around every tag, or making them larger than the CTA.

## 8. CTA grouping

### Anatomy

- One charcoal filled pill is the primary action.
- One neighboring outlined, white, or lightly translucent control is secondary.
- A small lime icon disk, status dot, or tiny lime badge may energize the group.

### Visual purpose

Makes choice feel decisive while retaining access to a social/community or exploratory action.

### Hierarchy

The filled charcoal pill leads. Green is normally an accent within the group, not a replacement for the primary dark fill.

### Spacing behavior

The group is tight—roughly a button-width relationship, not a spread-out toolbar. It wraps cleanly on mobile without losing primary-first order.

### When it works

For a single conversion moment or next action at the end of an editorial block.

### What breaks it

Two filled primary buttons, a neon-green full-fill primary button by default, overly large text, or icon-only controls with no visual hierarchy.

## 9. Navigation

### Anatomy

- Logo left.
- Compact primary links on the desktop center line.
- Small utility/social/status cluster and a dark pill CTA on the right.
- Hover flyouts range from a small single-column menu to a multi-column light mega-menu.
- On scroll, the desktop navigation becomes a rounded translucent floating rail.
- Tablet/mobile simplify to a menu trigger while retaining the critical action.

### Visual purpose

Creates a deliberately light top edge that can become a piece of floating equipment once the reader moves through the page.

### Hierarchy

Logo and final dark CTA bookend the navigation. Primary links remain deliberately small; the visual energy stays in the page content.

### Spacing behavior

Desktop links have generous horizontal gaps but little vertical height. Dropdowns sit close to their trigger with a small hover bridge so the menu feels attached rather than modal.

### When it works

For a broad information architecture that still needs an editorial landing page.

### What breaks it

A permanently opaque sticky header, oversized nav type, colored navigation backgrounds, bulky nav cards, or a mobile menu that discards the key action.

## 10. Split editorial / product composition

### Anatomy

- A compact, left-leaning text block and CTA group.
- A large artifact, globe, illustrated stage, or product proof placed beside or beneath it.
- The visual object frequently occupies more apparent area than the text.

### Visual purpose

Lets the page alternate between argument and proof without turning every section into a two-column feature list.

### Hierarchy

The headline starts the section; the object completes it. Avoid letting a long explanatory paragraph rival the proof stage.

### Spacing behavior

Use a major inter-column gap on desktop and stack to text-first then artifact on smaller screens. Maintain the artifact’s strong internal framing after stacking.

### When it works

For sections that need both a concise claim and a rich visual demonstration.

### What breaks it

Equal-width generic cards, symmetrical content density, or shrinking the object to make it fit an overly narrow text grid.

## 11. Elevated artifact treatment

### Anatomy

- White or dark material block.
- Thin border or inset edge.
- Soft low-contrast shadow.
- Moderate round corners.
- Sometimes a slight rotation, offset, crop, or dock/control cluster gives it physical presence.

### Visual purpose

Creates a curated, collectible object from otherwise flat interface material.

### Hierarchy

Elevation marks priority. Not every card should be elevated; the effect is strongest when reserved for proof objects, central metrics, or an interactive stage.

### Spacing behavior

Give an elevated artifact space to cast its shadow. Avoid crowding it with closely adjacent rules or competing cards.

### When it works

For visual evidence that needs to read as an object placed on the studio canvas.

### What breaks it

Strong dark drop shadows, glassy gradients, uniform elevation on every card, or radii so large that the object becomes toy-like.
